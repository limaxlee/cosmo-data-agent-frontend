Data agent frontend · admin area · design ideas

Three ways to lay out the admin area

The three mock-ups use the same invented people, visits and chats, and the app's own colours. In each one the navigation, the paging, the filters (press Enter after typing), choosing a person and the question before an action work. Nothing is saved: a reload brings the data back. The striped bar on top of each mock-up is a guide and not part of the design. It jumps to the views and shows the address a view would have.

A · Lists side by side

Five tabs, one list each: Waiting, Accounts, Access log, Sessions, Admins. The admin area opens on Waiting. A person has no view of their own: "their visits" is the access log with that person preselected.

Best at
Looking across everybody for a period. It is also the simplest to learn and to build: every view is one list with its filters.
Weakest at
Following one person. Their state, their visits and their chats are on three different tabs.

B · People first Recommended

The admin area opens on the people. The list is on the left with search and status filters. The right side shows the waiting people until a person is chosen, then that person: state, actions, visits, chats. The lists across everybody are separate views in the top bar.

Best at
The three most frequent jobs. Approving, finding one person and looking at what they did all happen on the opening screen.
Weakest at
It is the densest screen, and its address carries the most (list filters plus the chosen person, their tab and their dates). In a narrow window the person has to cover the list.
Addresses
/admin (shows People) · /admin/users, with the person as ?user=… · /admin/access-log · /admin/sessions · /admin/admins
Open mock-up B

C · Overview first

A side menu like the chat's, and a start page with the waiting people, six numbers (accounts, active, deactivated, admins, visits today, chats created today) and the latest visits and chats. Each person has a page of their own. The full lists are behind the menu.

Best at
Seeing at a glance what is going on today, and reading one person's whole situation on one page.
Weakest at
Everything except approving is one click further away. The start page also asks the backend about nine questions each time it loads, because each number is its own request.
Addresses
/admin (the overview) · /admin/users · /admin/users/{login ID} · /admin/access-log · /admin/sessions · /admin/admins

Typing a search text or a login ID is not counted. The confirming click of a question is counted. For a period, the count uses the "Today" or "Last 7 days" shortcut; choosing two days by hand is two clicks more.

Recommendation: B

The three jobs an admin does most often are about one person at a time: approve them, find them, see what they did. B is the only design where all three happen on the screen the admin area opens on. A and C are equal to B for approving and for looking across everybody, and behind it wherever a single person is concerned.

B costs more to build than A, because its address has to hold the list filters and the chosen person at the same time. That is a known amount of work, not a risk. If the simplest possible build matters more than the clicks, A is the one to take.

The same in all three

  • Status is a word with an icon and a colour: Active, Waiting, Deactivated. "Admin" is a separate mark. An admin who is not approved also gets the note "Has access as admin".
  • Every change asks first, in the person's terms. Your own account offers neither "Deactivate" nor "Remove admin".
  • "Only j.kim" is a removable mark that means this person exactly. A typed search is a different filter and also finds hj.kim.
  • Dates are whole days in the admin's time zone, both end days included, with shortcuts for today and the last seven days.
  • The Admins view lists the admins by themselves and takes a typed login ID, because an admin can exist before their account does (daeho.kwon in the mock-ups).
  • The day fields are the browser's own date fields, so no new package is needed. They show a day in the format of the browser's language.
  • Pages of 50, a Refresh button on every list, no timers.
